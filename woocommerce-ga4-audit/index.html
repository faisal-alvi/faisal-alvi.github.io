Why broken tracking costs you money

Most stores never notice. Orders still arrive, money still lands, and GA4 shows a number that looks roughly right. The damage shows up in your ads. Google Ads and Meta use purchase data to decide who to target and which campaigns to scale. If a share of real sales never reaches them, campaigns that work look weak and get cut. If purchases are counted twice, campaigns that don't work look profitable and keep spending. Either way, the budget follows bad numbers, and the reports you send to your team or your clients stop matching the store.

Signs your tracking is broken

  • GA4 purchase revenue is noticeably lower (or higher) than WooCommerce sales for the same dates
  • The same transaction ID shows up more than once in GA4
  • Orders paid with some payment methods barely appear in GA4, while others look fine
  • Conversions dropped after a plugin, theme, or WooCommerce update, but sales didn't
  • The numbers shifted after a cookie consent banner went live
  • Google Ads or Meta report a very different number of purchases than your store does
  • A tracking plugin, a GTM container, and a few hardcoded snippets are all sending ecommerce events, and nobody is sure which one is the real one

What the audit checks

  • Order-level reconciliation. Your WooCommerce orders matched against GA4 purchase events by transaction ID, for a recent period we agree on.
  • Missing purchases. Orders in WooCommerce with no matching GA4 event, broken down by payment method, device, and order status where the data allows.
  • Duplicate purchases. The same transaction recorded more than once, and where the extra hits come from.
  • Revenue mismatch. Differences in totals between the store and GA4, including how tax, shipping, currency, and refunds are handled.
  • Off-site payment redirects. Whether buyers who pay on a gateway's hosted page actually make it back to the thank-you page that fires the purchase event.
  • Consent and blockers. Whether your consent banner, Consent Mode setup, or ad blockers are stopping the purchase tag from firing.
  • Every tracking source. All the plugins, GTM containers, and hardcoded scripts sending ecommerce events, and where they overlap.
  • Recent changes. Plugin, theme, and WooCommerce updates that line up with when the numbers went off.

FAQ

What access do you need?

Read-only (Viewer) access to your GA4 property, plus either a WooCommerce order export (CSV) for the period we agree on or a read-only WooCommerce REST API key. If Google Tag Manager is involved, read access to the container helps too. I don't need a WordPress admin login for the audit.

Will you change anything on my store?

No. The audit is read-only. Nothing gets installed, edited, or deleted on your site.

How long does it take?

Usually 3 to 5 business days from the point access is in place. Very high order volumes, or several stores at once, can take a little longer, and I'll tell you upfront if that's the case.

Is it really free?

Yes. No card, no invoice, and no obligation to hire me afterward. It's the quickest way for both of us to see whether there's a real problem worth fixing. If your numbers turn out to match, that's a good result, and the report will say so.

What happens after the audit?

You keep the report either way. If you want the fixes done, I'll send a fixed quote based on what the audit found. If you'd rather have your own developer or agency handle it, the report is written so they can.

What counts as a "small fix" on a monitoring plan?

A contained fix to the tracking itself, such as a tag that stopped firing after an update, a duplicate event source, or a consent setting blocking the purchase event. Tracking Watch includes up to 1 hour of fixes a month, and Pro and Agency include up to 2 hours a month per store. Larger work, like rebuilding a GTM setup or changing how a payment gateway returns buyers to your store, is quoted separately before anything starts.

Can I cancel a monitoring plan?

Yes. Plans are month to month with no long-term contract, and you can cancel any time.
